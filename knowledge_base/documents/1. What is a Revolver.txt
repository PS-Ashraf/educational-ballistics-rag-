1. What is a Revolver?
A revolver is a handgun with a rotating cylinder that holds multiple cartridges, typically 5–8 rounds. Each pull of the trigger rotates a fresh chamber into line with the barrel.

Revolver types
Single-action (SA): You must manually cock the hammer before each shot. Example: old Colt Single Action Army.

Double-action (DA): Pulling the trigger cocks and releases the hammer. Can also be fired single-action.

Double-action only (DAO): Hammer is internal or bobbed; every shot is double-action. Common in concealed-carry snub-nose revolvers.

Common revolver calibers
.22 LR – cheap training, small game

.38 Special – classic self-defense/police round

.357 Magnum – powerful defense/hunting; can also fire .38 Special

.44 Magnum – hunting, large animal defense

.45 Colt – historical, hunting loads

Pros and cons of revolvers
Pros	Cons
Very simple mechanism	Low capacity, usually 5–8 rounds
Extremely reliable	Slower to reload than a magazine-fed pistol
No magazine to fail or lose	Wider cylinder can be harder to conceal
Can fire powerful magnum cartridges	Heavy double-action trigger can hurt accuracy for beginners
Works well in contact shots	Spent casings stay in cylinder until manually ejected
Good revolver uses: training, home defense, concealed carry, backup gun, hunting, and sport shooting.

2. Major Firearm Types
Firearms are generally divided into:

Handguns
Revolvers – rotating cylinder

Semi-automatic pistols – use a magazine, fire one shot per trigger pull, automatically reload

Single-shot pistols – one round at a time

Rifles
Long-barreled firearms designed for accuracy and power.

Bolt-action – manually operated, very accurate, common for hunting/precision

Lever-action – lever below the trigger, classic hunting rifles

Pump-action – sliding forearm, common in shotguns and some rifles

Semi-automatic – one shot per trigger pull, self-loading

Fully automatic / selective fire – military rifles capable of burst/full-auto

Shotguns
Smoothbore or partly rifled firearms firing shells containing multiple pellets or a single slug.

Pump-action – most common for home defense

Semi-automatic – faster follow-up shots

Break-action – single or double barrel, simple and reliable

Bolt-action shotguns – rare, usually for hunting

Submachine Guns and Machine Guns
Submachine gun (SMG): pistol-caliber fully automatic weapon

Machine gun: rifle-caliber fully automatic weapon, usually military/LE only

3. Ammunition and Bullets
A complete cartridge/round has four basic parts:

Case – brass, steel, or aluminum container

Primer – ignites the powder when struck

Propellant – smokeless powder

Projectile/bullet – the part that leaves the barrel

Rimfire vs Centerfire
Rimfire: primer is in the rim. Common in .22 LR, .17 HMR. Generally lower power.

Centerfire: primer is in the center of the case base. Used in almost all serious handgun, rifle, and shotgun slug loads.

Common bullet types
Type	Design	Typical use
Full Metal Jacket (FMJ)	Lead core fully covered by copper jacket	Military, target practice
Jacketed Hollow Point (JHP)	Hollow tip expands on impact	Self-defense, law enforcement
Soft Point (SP)	Exposed lead tip for controlled expansion	Hunting
Ballistic Tip	Plastic tip improves aerodynamics and expansion	Hunting, long-range
Frangible	Breaks up on hard surfaces, reduces ricochet	Training, close-range shooting
Armor Piercing	Hard steel/tungsten core	Military/LE, often restricted
Tracer	Ignites to show flight path	Military, some ranges ban it
Subsonic	Below speed of sound, quieter with suppressor	Specialized use
Shotgun ammunition
Birdshot – many small pellets; birds, clay targets

Buckshot – larger pellets; self-defense, medium game. “00 buck” is common.

Slug – single large projectile; big game, defense, law enforcement

Muzzle energy (ft-lb) = (bullet weight in grains × velocity² in fps) ÷ 450,240

4. Ballistics Basics
Ballistics is the science of projectile motion. It is divided into three parts:

A. Internal Ballistics
What happens inside the firearm from primer ignition to bullet exit.

Primer ignites powder

Powder burns, generating high pressure

Pressure forces the bullet down the barrel

Barrel length, chamber pressure, bullet weight, and powder charge affect velocity

Longer barrel generally gives more velocity, but only up to a point

B. External Ballistics
The bullet’s flight from muzzle to target.

Gravity causes drop

Air resistance/drag slows the bullet

Wind drifts the bullet sideways

Spin drift from rifling causes slight lateral movement

Ballistic coefficient (BC) measures how well a bullet resists drag

Zeroing adjusts sights so the bullet hits point of aim at a known distance

C. Terminal Ballistics
What happens when the bullet hits the target.

Penetration – how deep it goes

Expansion – how much the bullet widens

Fragmentation – bullet breaks apart

Energy transfer – how much kinetic energy is dumped into the target

Temporary cavity and permanent cavity – tissue damage effects

For self-defense, the FBI standard is generally 12 to 18 inches of penetration in ballistic gelatin, with reliable expansion. Shot placement and penetration matter more than raw caliber.

5. Global Firearms Laws and Regulations
There is no single world gun law. Every country has its own rules. Some countries allow civilian ownership with licenses; others ban private ownership almost entirely.

Key regulatory approaches used by governments
Licensing – require a permit to own/possess

Registration – record firearm serial numbers and owners

Background checks – criminal, mental health, domestic violence checks

Waiting periods – delay purchase

Safe storage laws – require locking devices/safes

Magazine capacity limits – restrict rounds

“Assault weapon” bans – restrict certain semi-automatic features

Red flag laws – temporary removal from dangerous persons

Buyback programs – government purchases guns from civilians

Import/export controls – track international movement

International Treaties and Legal Articles
Some important international instruments:

Article 8 – Prohibition on transfer in certain circumstances

Article 11 – Record keeping

Article 12 – Reporting

Protocol against Illicit Manufacturing of and Trafficking in Firearms.

Article 12 – Confiscation, seizure, disposal

Multilateral export control regime for conventional arms and dual-use goods.

US Federal Law Articles

26 U.S.C. Chapter 53 – National Firearms Act, machine guns, suppressors, short-barreled rifles/shotguns

These are examples only. Legal texts are complex and must be read in full.

6. Which Weapon Is Good for Which Purpose?
There is no single best gun. The right firearm depends on purpose, local law, user training, and physical ability.

Purpose	Recommended Firearm Type	Common Calibers / Ammo	Notes
Home defense	Pump-action shotgun or pistol-caliber carbine or full-size pistol	12 gauge 00 buck, 9mm JHP, .223/5.56	Shotgun is intimidating and effective; pistol is maneuverable
Concealed carry	Compact/subcompact semi-auto pistol or snub-nose revolver	9mm, .380 ACP, .38 Special	Reliability and concealment matter most
Target shooting / plinking	.22 LR rifle or pistol	.22 LR	Cheap, low recoil, excellent for training
Small game hunting	.22 LR rifle or 20 gauge shotgun	.22 LR, 20 gauge birdshot	Ethical, quiet, low meat damage
Bird hunting	12 or 20 gauge shotgun	Birdshot	Gauge depends on bird size and range
Deer-size game	Bolt-action rifle	.308 Win, 6.5 Creedmoor, .270 Win, .30-06	Enough energy and accuracy for ethical kills
Large/dangerous game	Large bolt-action rifle or double rifle	.375 H&H, .338 Win Mag, .416 Rigby	Legal minimums often apply
Long-range precision	Heavy-barrel bolt-action rifle	6.5 Creedmoor, .300 PRC, .338 Lapua Magnum	High ballistic coefficient, quality optics
Law enforcement patrol	Semi-auto pistol, patrol rifle, shotgun	9mm, 5.56 NATO, 12 gauge	Standardized, reliable, controllable
Military infantry	Assault rifle, sidearm, DMR	5.56 NATO, 9mm, 7.62 NATO	Selective fire, magazines, logistics
Training new shooters	.22 LR rifle or full-size 9mm	.22 LR, 9mm	Low recoil builds fundamentals
7. Safety and Legal Responsibilities
Regardless of country, basic firearms safety rules are universal:

Treat every firearm as if it is loaded

Never point a firearm at anything you are not willing to destroy

Keep your finger off the trigger until ready to shoot

Know your target and what is behind it

Legal responsibilities
Obtain required licenses/permits

Register firearms where required

Follow storage and transport laws

Never carry across borders without permission

Do not sell/transfer to prohibited persons

Report lost or stolen firearms

8. Bottom Line
A revolver is a reliable handgun with a rotating cylinder, good for training, defense, and hunting.

Ballistics is the science of how bullets behave inside the gun, in flight, and on impact.

Ammunition comes in many calibers, bullet types, and power levels for different jobs.

Gun laws differ dramatically around the world, from constitutional right to near-total ban.

The best firearm depends on your purpose, training, and legal environment.

If you are considering buying or using a firearm, contact a licensed instructor or attorney in your country. Firearms are serious tools requiring training, respect, and legal compliance.
